# Writing Hairy Popsicles

How the show talks. Skits are 30 to 60 seconds, in the space between
Salad Fingers (soft, slow, lonely, polite about awful things) and Ren & Stimpy
(one abrasive, in-your-face spike). The randomness and timing of MeatCanyon and
The Big Lez Show are in there too.

## The voice

- **Gentle delivery, disturbing content.** The worst things are said the most
  politely: "I made them out of my old scabs. Took me all summer." Calm
  is creepier than shouting, so shouting is saved for once an episode.
- **One spike, then quiet.** Build softly, snap to a scream (`screaming,
  punch, shake`, an extreme close-up), then drop straight back to a whisper.
  The quiet after it is where the laugh is.
- **The mundane, made wrong.** Job interviews, dates, gifts, bedtime. Ordinary
  rituals with something rotten in them. Never explain the wrongness.
- **Oddly specific.** Not "a long time" but "since nineteen ninety-four". Not
  "a mess" but "into a birthday party". The specific detail is the joke.
- **Each one's deformity is their bad luck, never their punchline.** They live
  with it. They're tired of it. They've made arrangements.

## The dialogue

- **Start mid-moment.** No hellos, no setup. The first line is already halfway
  into something.
- **Nobody answers the question.** People talk past each other, each chasing
  their own fixation. The answer, if it comes, comes three lines later
  ("...years.").
- **Under the line.** Conehead is lonely; he never says so, he gives a gift.
  Melter is moved; she says "is that for me?" about a pimple.
- **Rhythm per character.** Big Eye: short, flat, one word if he can. Conehead:
  soft, sing-song, too close. Droopy: trails off... Sweats: too fast, sorry,
  sorry, oversharing. Snoot: slow, pompous, demanding. Shiv: short evasions.
  Pimples: fragments that crack. Melter: deadpan poetry. Red Eye: a flat
  whisper, to himself.
- **Fragments and cut-offs.** People stop, restart, interrupt themselves. A
  line ending in `—` is cut off: the next line comes straight in. A line
  ending in `...` lingers.
- **Threes with a turn.** One sheep, two sheep, three sheep has my mum's face.
- **End on a button.** A small, quiet, wrong last line, ideally said over
  someone else's face (`off`). Not a punchline; a door left open.

## On the page

- Say how lines are said, in brackets: `(softly)`, `(flat)`, `(whispering,
  delighted)`. A line without a note gets the character's usual manner
  (`manner` in `cast.json`).
- Give silences room: `> NAME (1.5s, still)` is a free beat, and the music
  swells into it. Most episodes want two or three.
- Put lines over reactions: `NAME (off): ...` plays the line over whoever
  was on screen. A reaction is often funnier than the face saying it.
- Keep every line short. If a line has a comma in the middle, it can
  probably be two lines, or a line and a beat.
- 8 to 12 lines is about 40 seconds.
